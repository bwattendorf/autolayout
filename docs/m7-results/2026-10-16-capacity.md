# Routing capacity (2026-10-16): the arithmetic, and what it says about m1/candidate4

New: `route::capacity` and `autolayout capacity PCB [--part REF] [--min-pads N] [--all] [--plane L=N ...]`.

## The arithmetic
- A track's centre-line keeps `track/2 + clearance` (0.2286 mm at 0.1524 / 0.1524) from any copper edge; two centre-lines are `track + clearance` (0.3048 mm) apart.
- A gap of `g` mm between two copper edges holds `floor((g - 0.4572) / 0.3048) + 1` tracks per layer (0 if `g` < 0.4572). `gap_for_tracks(n)` is the inverse: 0.4572 + (n - 1) x 0.3048; `gap_for_nets(nets, layers)` is the gap two facing parts need for `nets` nets on `layers` signal layers (the nets spread evenly). These are the numbers a placer can use as a minimum gap between facing parts.
- A closed ring around a part: every net with a pad inside and a pad outside must cross it. Free stretches of the ring (where a centre-line clears every foreign pad on that layer and the board edge) hold `floor(L / 0.3048) + 1` tracks per layer. Demand above capacity means no router can finish. `strict` capacity lets every foreign pad block; `loose` lets a net's own pads not block it (an upper bound, so demand above `loose` is a proof). Plane nets are left out of the demand (they need a via, not a crossing). Not counted: vias, oblique crossings, the choice of other loops. It is an upper bound on capacity, not a router.

## Tests
`capacity::tests`: the gap formula and its inverse (n = 1..7, and 0.4571 mm holds 0 while 0.4572 holds 1), the ring run counting (including a stretch that wraps), and a synthetic walled ring against an open one. `ci.sh` result below.

No part with at least 8 netted pads has a tight, over-capacity or impossible ring (J10, U8, U6, U5 are all at 5-9 crossing nets against 170-250 tracks of room).
**Reading:** around U1 there is five to six times the room the nets need, at every distance. Total room around parts is not what stops the router on this board. What stops it is local: narrow channels (for example about 1.2 mm between U1's west edge and U2, room for about 3 tracks per layer) that several nets pick at once, and a grid router that cannot use the room it has. A ring is a weak cut; this does not prove the board routes, only that nothing here proves it cannot. It also means the service-placement boards in this corpus are not short of copper room around their ICs; the placer can use `gap_for_nets` for the gaps between facing parts.
